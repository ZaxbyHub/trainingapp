# Security Basics

Security is everyone's responsibility. This guide covers the practices every employee must
follow on day one.

## Passwords and credentials

Passwords must be at least 12 characters long and must not reuse a password from another
service. Password managers provided by the company are the recommended way to store them.
Never share credentials in chat, email, or tickets; support staff will never ask for your
password.

## Phishing and reporting

Suspicious emails should be reported with the "Report phishing" button in the mail client,
or forwarded to the security team mailbox. Report a suspected phishing click within one
hour — fast reporting is the single most effective containment step and is never punished.
Simulated phishing exercises run quarterly.

## Data classification

Data falls into four classification tiers: public, internal, confidential, and restricted.
Confidential data may only be stored on encrypted, managed devices. Restricted data
(customer records, payment data) requires explicit written approval before export and must
never leave approved systems.

## Devices and physical space

Full-disk encryption is mandatory on every laptop and is verified by the device management
agent monthly. Badge tailgating into the office is prohibited; if you forget your badge,
sign in at reception. Follow a clean-desk standard: lock your screen when you leave and
store printed documents in the locked cabinets.

## Incident response

Security incidents are reported to the incident hotline. The response team commits to a
first response within one hour of a report. Do not attempt to investigate a suspected
compromise yourself; preserve the device state and hand it to the response team.
